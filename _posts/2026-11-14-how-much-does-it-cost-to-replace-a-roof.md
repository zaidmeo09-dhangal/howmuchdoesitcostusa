## Repair vs replace: how to decide

<p>Three rules from <a href="https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-roof-replacement-cost.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Angi</a> help you decide whether to repair or replace.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The 50% rule.</strong> Replace if the repair costs 50% or more of a full replacement. On a $9,180 roof, that line is $4,590: 50% of $9,180 = $4,590.</li>
<li><strong>The age rule.</strong> Replace if the roof is 20 or more years old, even if the damage looks small, because more failures usually follow.</li>
<li><strong>The damage rule.</strong> Replace if damage covers more than 20% of the roof surface, because patching that much roof rarely holds.</li>
</ul>

<p>If none of the three rules points to replacement, a repair is usually the better spend. Get the roofer to put the repair price and the replacement price on the same quote so you can test the 50% rule yourself.</p>

## Does homeowners insurance pay for a new roof?

<p>Storm damage is often covered, while wear and age are usually excluded. Hail and wind damage from a single storm is the usual covered case, while a roof that failed because it is old, or because leaks were left alone, is usually not covered. You pay the deductible first, and the insurer pays the covered cost above it. File the claim before work starts, and keep photos of the damage.</p>

## Why roof averages disagree

<p>Published averages for the same job do not match, because each source counts different roofs, materials, and regions. <a href="https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-roof-replacement-cost.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Angi</a> reports an average of $9,611 from 32,999 verified projects, HomeGuide reports about $10,800, Fixr reports about $10,000, and <a href="https://www.thisoldhouse.com/roofing/new-roof-cost" target="_blank" rel="noopener">This Old House</a> reports a budget of $10,319 for a 2,000 sq ft asphalt roof. Use the range for your roof size and material, not a single national average cost of roof replacement, and compare quotes for the same scope.</p>

## What affects the price

### Roof size and pitch

Bigger roofs need more material and more labor, so the price rises with every square foot. A steep pitch also costs more, because the crew works slower and needs extra safety gear.

### Material choice

Asphalt shingle is the cheapest common material, metal costs more, and clay tile costs the most in these sample ranges. Within each material, thicker and longer lasting grades cost more than the basic grade. Within asphalt, 3-tab shingles cost $3 to $5 per sq ft and architectural shingles cost $4 to $7 per sq ft. Architectural shingles are thicker and longer lasting, so they are usually the better value. Metal costs more up front but lasts 40 to 70 years, and clay tile costs the most and needs a roof frame strong enough to carry its weight.

### Tear-off and old layers

Removing the old roof, hauling it away, and disposing of it adds labor and dump fees. A roof with two old layers costs more to tear off than a roof with one layer.

### Location and labor rates

Labor rates vary across the USA, and big cities usually cost more than small towns for the same roof. Local building codes can also add cost, for example when ice and water shield is required. Labor is the largest line after materials: in the worked example on this page, labor is $4,250 of the $11,125 total. How much does it cost to replace a roof in Boston? It depends on Boston labor rates and local permit fees, which differ from the national sample ranges on this page.

### Permits and disposal

Most US cities require a permit for a full roof replacement, and the permit fee is part of the job. Disposal fees for the old roofing also vary by area, and they rise with the weight of the old material.

## Hidden costs to check on every quote

<p>A low headline price often hides work that shows up later. Check every quote for these items, and ask for each one in writing before you compare totals: deck replacement beyond minor repairs, with the per-sheet price in the contract; skylight and chimney flashing; fascia and soffit repairs; disposal of the old roofing; and the permit fee.</p>

## How to save money

<ul class="checklist">
<li><strong>Get 3 written quotes.</strong> Compare the same material, the same scope, and the same warranty, not just the bottom line price.</li>
<li><strong>Schedule in the off season.</strong> Late fall and winter are usually cheaper, because roofers have fewer jobs booked.</li>
<li><strong>Compare material grades.</strong> A mid grade architectural shingle often lasts longer than the cheapest 3-tab shingle, and it can be better value over time.</li>
<li><strong>Ask about the warranty.</strong> A longer manufacturer warranty can be worth a small price rise, and it can also help when you sell the home.</li>
<li><strong>Ask about payment plans.</strong> Many roofers offer monthly payments, and a home equity loan is another common way to pay for a roof. Compare the total cost of any financing, not just the monthly payment.</li>
<li><strong>Avoid storm chasers.</strong> Door to door roofers after a storm often charge more and leave town fast, so use a local roofer with a real address and reviews.</li>
</ul>

## How to hire a roofer

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Measure your roof and note its condition.</strong> Note the roof size, the pitch, the material, and any leaks, soft spots, or missing shingles.</li>
<li><strong>Get 3 written quotes.</strong> Ask each roofer for the same material and scope, and ask for the permit, disposal, and warranty to be listed in writing.</li>
<li><strong>Verify license and insurance.</strong> Check the roofer license for your state, and ask for proof of liability insurance and workers compensation cover.</li>
<li><strong>Check reviews and past jobs.</strong> Read recent reviews, ask for addresses of recent jobs you can drive past, and ask for references you can call.</li>
<li><strong>Sign a detailed contract.</strong> The contract should list the material, the brand, the color, the price, the start date, the payment schedule, the per-sheet decking price, and both warranties: workmanship and manufacturer material.</li>
</ol>

## Roof cost by material per square foot

<p>Per square foot prices show why material choice matters, and they set the cost to replace a roof as much as the roof size does. 3-tab asphalt runs $3 to $5 per sq ft and architectural asphalt $4 to $7 per sq ft, so the calculator default of $5.10 per sq ft sits inside the architectural range. Metal and clay tile cost more up front, but they last much longer: an asphalt shingle roof lasts 20 to 30 years, and a metal roof lasts 40 to 70 years.</p>

## Roof cost per year of ownership

<p>The upfront cost of roof replacement is only half the story. An asphalt roof at $9,180 costs $459 a year over 20 years and $306 a year over 30 years. A metal roof at $17,100 costs about $428 a year over 40 years and about $244 a year over 70 years. Metal costs about $7,920 more than asphalt up front, yet it can cost less per year because it lasts about twice as long. The per year math favors the longer lasting material, but only if you plan to stay in the home long enough to use that life. If you plan to sell in a few years, a mid grade architectural shingle often gives the better value.</p>

## Roof install day and inspection checklist

<ul class="checklist">
<li><strong>Clear the driveway and the yard.</strong> Move cars, patio furniture, and plants away from the house before the crew arrives.</li>
<li><strong>Protect the attic and the walls.</strong> Cover stored items in the attic, and take pictures off the walls, because hammering shakes the house.</li>
<li><strong>Check the deck once the old roof is off.</strong> Ask the roofer to show you any soft or rotten decking before it is covered, and confirm the per-sheet decking price in the contract.</li>
<li><strong>Check the flashing, vents, and ridge cap.</strong> These are part of a full job, and they are the spots where leaks start if they are skipped.</li>
<li><strong>Walk the yard after cleanup.</strong> Check for nails with the roofer, and confirm the disposal of the old roofing is complete.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the warranty papers.</strong> Save both warranties, workmanship and manufacturer material, with the contract and the permit.</li>
</ul>

## Signs your roof needs replacing

<p>Age and damage are the two clearest signs, and the repair vs replace rules on this page use both: replace if the roof is 20 or more years old, and replace if damage covers more than 20 percent of the roof surface. Look for the visible signs too. Missing, cracked, or curling shingles, granules in the gutters, daylight through the roof boards in the attic, and water stains on ceilings or walls all point to a roof that is failing. A roof that leaks in several places, or one that has been patched again and again, usually costs more to keep repairing than to replace. If the damage is small and the roof is young, a repair is usually the better spend. Get the roofer to put the repair price and the replacement price on the same quote, then test the 50 percent rule yourself: replace if the repair costs 50 percent or more of a full replacement, which is $4,590 on a $9,180 roof.</p>

## Roof area is bigger than the house

<p>Roofers price by roof area, not by home floor area, and the roof is always bigger than the footprint of the house. The roof extends past the exterior walls, and the pitch adds surface area, so a 2,000 sq ft house typically has a roof of 22 to 28 squares, where a square is 100 sq ft. A low pitch adds a little area, a steep pitch adds a lot, and a complex roof with valleys, dormers, and chimneys adds more still. That is why two homes with the same floor area can get very different roof quotes. Ask the roofer for the measured roof area in squares on the written quote, so you can check the per square foot price against the $5.10 per sq ft used for architectural asphalt on this page.</p>

## Overlay or tear-off: why tear-off usually wins

<p>A new roof can sometimes go over the old one, but a full tear-off is usually better. A tear-off lets the roofer check the deck, fix hidden damage, and install the new roof on a clean surface. An overlay hides the deck, so rotten wood stays hidden, and it adds a second layer that costs $1 to $2 per sq ft extra to remove later. An overlay can also shorten the life of the new roof and can void the manufacturer warranty, which is why many reputable roofers refuse overlays. If the old roof has two layers already, a tear-off is the only sensible choice, because a roof with two old layers costs more to tear off than a roof with one layer, and a third layer is not a sound roof.</p>

## Frequently asked questions

<details class="faq"><summary>How long does a new roof last?</summary><p>An asphalt shingle roof lasts 20 to 30 years, and a metal roof lasts 40 to 70 years. The real life depends on the material grade, the install quality, and the local weather.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>Does it need a permit?</summary><p>Most US cities require a permit for a full roof replacement. A good roofer pulls the permit for you, and the permit fee is usually listed in the written quote.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>Can a new roof go over the old one?</summary><p>Sometimes, but a full tear-off is usually better. A tear-off lets the roofer check the deck, fix hidden damage, and install the new roof on a clean surface.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>When is it cheapest?</summary><p>Late fall and winter are usually the cheapest times, because roofers have fewer jobs booked. Summer and the weeks after a big storm are the busiest and the priciest.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>What is not included?</summary><p>Gutters, skylights, and structural repairs are usually not included in a standard roof quote. Ask each roofer to list these items, so you know what the price does and does not cover.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>Does homeowners insurance pay for a new roof?</summary><p>Storm damage is often covered, while wear and age are usually excluded. You pay the deductible first, and the insurer pays the covered cost above it.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does roof pitch add to the cost?</summary><p>WhatHomeCosts puts a 4:12 to 8:12 pitch at base to 5 to 10% more, 8:12 to 12:12 at about 15 to 25% more, and 12:12 and over at up to about 30% more. On a $9,180 roof, 30% more is $9,180 x 1.30 = $11,934.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does a roof cost per year?</summary><p>An asphalt roof at $9,180 costs $459 a year over 20 years and $306 a year over 30 years. A metal roof at $17,100 costs about $428 a year over 40 years and about $244 a year over 70 years.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How do I know my roof needs replacing?</summary><p>Look for missing, cracked, or curling shingles, granules in the gutters, daylight in the attic, and water stains inside. Replace if the roof is 20 or more years old, or if damage covers more than 20 percent of the roof surface.</p></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What is the cost to replace a roof?</summary><p>A typical 1,800 sq ft asphalt shingle roof costs about $9,180 to replace, with a sample range of $7,528 to $11,475. Metal costs $13,500 to $21,000 with a typical total of $17,100, and clay tile costs $16,000 to $25,000 with a typical total of $21,600.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does an asphalt roof replacement cost?</summary><p>An asphalt roof replacement costs $5.10 per sq ft at the calculator default for architectural asphalt, so a 1,800 sq ft roof costs about $9,180 typical. 3-tab asphalt costs $3 to $5 per sq ft and architectural asphalt costs $4 to $7 per sq ft.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does it cost to get a roof replaced?</summary><p>The cost to have a roof replaced is the same as the cost to replace a roof. A typical 1,800 sq ft asphalt shingle roof costs about $9,180, with a sample range of $7,528 to $11,475. Use the Roof Replacement Cost Calculator to price your roof size and material.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does it cost to replace a metal roof?</summary><p>A metal roof costs $13,500 to $21,000 for a 1,800 sq ft roof, with a typical total of $17,100. Metal costs more up front than asphalt, but a metal roof lasts 40 to 70 years.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does it cost to replace a shingle roof?</summary><p>An asphalt shingle roof costs $7,528 to $11,475 for a 1,800 sq ft roof, with a typical total of $9,180. 3-tab asphalt costs $3 to $5 per sq ft and architectural asphalt costs $4 to $7 per sq ft.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does it cost to replace a commercial roof?</summary><p>The prices on this page are for residential roofs. Commercial roofs use different materials and systems and are priced per project, so ask a commercial roofer for a separate written quote.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does it cost to replace a flat roof?</summary><p>The prices on this page are for pitched roofs with shingles, metal, or tile. Flat roofs use membrane systems and are priced differently, so ask your roofer for a separate written quote.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How much does it cost to replace a garage roof?</summary><p>A garage roof is priced by the same per square foot rates as a house roof, so a small garage roof costs less than a full house roof. Architectural asphalt runs $4 to $7 per sq ft, so multiply your garage roof area by that rate for a rough idea, then get a written quote.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>How long does it take to replace a roof?</summary><p>Most single-family asphalt roofs take 2 to 3 days with a crew of 4 to 6 roofers. Larger, steeper, or more complex roofs, and metal or tile roofs, take longer.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>What does a roof cost per square?</summary><p>One roofing square is 100 sq ft of roof surface. A typical 1,800 sq ft asphalt roof is 18 squares at about $9,180, which works out to about $510 per square. A metal roof at $17,100 typical is about $950 per square.</p></details>
<details class="faq"><summary>What warranty should a new roof have?</summary><p>A new roof should come with two warranties: a workmanship warranty from the roofer and a manufacturer material warranty. Get both in writing in the contract, with the length and what each one covers, and keep the papers with the permit.</p></details>
