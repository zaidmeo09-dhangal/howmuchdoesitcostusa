## What affects the price

### Roof size and pitch

Bigger roofs need more material and more labor, so the price rises with every square foot. A steep pitch also costs more, because the crew works slower and needs extra safety gear.

### Material choice

Asphalt shingle is the cheapest common material, metal costs more, and clay tile costs the most in these sample ranges. Within each material, thicker and longer lasting grades cost more than the basic grade.

### Tear-off and old layers

Removing the old roof, hauling it away, and disposing of it adds labor and dump fees. A roof with two old layers costs more to tear off than a roof with one layer.

### Location and labor rates

Labor rates vary across the USA, and big cities usually cost more than small towns for the same roof. Local building codes can also add cost, for example when ice and water shield is required.

### Permits and disposal

Most US cities require a permit for a full roof replacement, and the permit fee is part of the job. Disposal fees for the old roofing also vary by area, and they rise with the weight of the old material.

## How to save money

<ul class="checklist">
<li><strong>Get 3 written quotes.</strong> Compare the same material, the same scope, and the same warranty, not just the bottom line price.</li>
<li><strong>Schedule in the off season.</strong> Late fall and winter are usually cheaper, because roofers have fewer jobs booked.</li>
<li><strong>Compare material grades.</strong> A mid grade architectural shingle often lasts longer than the cheapest 3-tab shingle, and it can be better value over time.</li>
<li><strong>Ask about the warranty.</strong> A longer manufacturer warranty can be worth a small price rise, and it can also help when you sell the home.</li>
<li><strong>Avoid storm chasers.</strong> Door to door roofers after a storm often charge more and leave town fast, so use a local roofer with a real address and reviews.</li>
</ul>

## How to hire a roofer

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Measure your roof and note its condition.</strong> Note the roof size, the pitch, the material, and any leaks, soft spots, or missing shingles.</li>
<li><strong>Get 3 written quotes.</strong> Ask each roofer for the same material and scope, and ask for the permit, disposal, and warranty to be listed in writing.</li>
<li><strong>Verify license and insurance.</strong> Check the roofer license for your state, and ask for proof of liability insurance and workers compensation cover.</li>
<li><strong>Check reviews and past jobs.</strong> Read recent reviews, ask for addresses of recent jobs you can drive past, and ask for references you can call.</li>
<li><strong>Sign a detailed contract.</strong> The contract should list the material, the brand, the color, the price, the start date, the payment schedule, and the warranty terms.</li>
</ol>
