## What affects the price

### Home size

Bigger homes need bigger systems, and bigger systems cost more. A 2,500 sq ft home needs about twice the cooling power of a 1,000 sq ft home, so the price rises with the size of the home.

### System size and SEER rating

System size is measured in tons, and the SEER rating measures efficiency. A higher SEER system costs about 15 to 30 percent more up front, but it uses less power and can cost less to run each summer.

### Ductwork condition

Good ducts in the right size can stay, but leaky, undersized, or damaged ducts need repair or replacement. Duct work is one of the biggest cost swings in a central air job, and it is often found only after the installer inspects the home.

### Location and labor rates

Labor rates vary across the USA, and big cities usually cost more than small towns for the same install. Hot states also have more demand in summer, which can push prices up in the busy season.

### Electrical panel

An older home may need a panel upgrade or a new circuit for the outdoor unit. That work is usually priced apart from the AC install, and it can add a large amount to the total job.

## How to save money

<ul class="checklist">
<li><strong>Get 3 written quotes.</strong> Compare the same system size, the same SEER rating, and the same scope, not just the bottom line price.</li>
<li><strong>Size it correctly.</strong> An oversized system costs more to buy and short cycles, which wastes power and wears the system out faster.</li>
<li><strong>Compare SEER payback.</strong> A higher SEER system costs more now, so ask each installer to show the yearly power saving before you pay the extra.</li>
<li><strong>Schedule in the off season.</strong> Fall and winter installs are often cheaper, because installers have fewer jobs booked.</li>
<li><strong>Check rebates and tax credits.</strong> Federal, state, and utility rebates can cut the cost of an efficient system, so check what is open before you buy.</li>
</ul>

## How to hire an AC installer

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Ask for a load calculation.</strong> A good installer measures the home, checks insulation and windows, and sizes the system from the numbers, not from a guess.</li>
<li><strong>Get 3 written quotes.</strong> Ask each installer for the same system size and SEER rating, with ducts, permits, and warranty listed in writing.</li>
<li><strong>Verify license and insurance.</strong> Check the HVAC license for your state, and ask for proof of liability insurance and workers compensation cover.</li>
<li><strong>Compare warranties.</strong> Check the warranty on the equipment, the labor warranty, and what is needed to keep each warranty valid.</li>
<li><strong>Get everything in writing.</strong> The contract should list the brand, the model, the size, the SEER rating, the price, the start date, and the warranty terms.</li>
</ol>
