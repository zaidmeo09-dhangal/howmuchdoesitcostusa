  <h2>What affects the price</h2>
  <h3>Fence length</h3>
  <p>Cost scales with length, so an accurate measurement matters more than any other input. Walk the boundary with a tape or measuring wheel, and include the space for every gate.</p>
  <h3>Material</h3>
  <p>Wood is the cheapest privacy option up front, vinyl costs more but needs less care, and chain link is the cheapest per foot. Your choice sets both the up front price and the long term upkeep.</p>
  <h3>Fence height and style</h3>
  <p>Taller fences and privacy styles use more material and cost more than short picket fences. A 6 ft privacy fence costs more than a 4 ft picket fence of the same length.</p>
  <h3>Gates and slopes</h3>
  <p>Gates add hardware and labor, and sloped ground needs stepped panels and extra posts. Each gate can add a few hundred dollars, so count them before you ask for a quote.</p>
  <h3>Permits and surveys</h3>
  <p>Many cities require a fence permit, and a survey may be needed to confirm the boundary. Permit fees vary by city, and a survey is often a separate cost.</p>

  <h2>How we estimate</h2>
  <p>We multiply fence length by a per foot rate for each material, then set low at 63 percent and high at 162 percent of the typical price. The result is a sample estimated USA range for planning.</p>
  <p>Your final price depends on height, gates, ground slope, and local labor rates, so always confirm the price in writing before work starts.</p>

  <h2>Hiring a pro checklist</h2>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li>Get 3 written quotes and compare them line by line</li>
    <li>Check the property line before work starts</li>
    <li>Ask about the permit and who pulls it</li>
    <li>Confirm gate count and hardware in writing</li>
    <li>Ask about the warranty on materials and labor</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Budget tips</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Choose wood for the lowest up front cost</li>
    <li>Share a boundary fence cost with a neighbor when rules allow</li>
    <li>Keep the design simple with fewer gates</li>
    <li>Get quotes in the off season when installers are less busy</li>
  </ul>
