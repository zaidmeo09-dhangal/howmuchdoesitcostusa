  <h2>Our mission</h2>
  <p>Our mission is simple: give every American a straight, honest price before they hire someone. A roof, a fence, a move, or a repaint should not start with a guess or a sales call. It should start with a number you can check, a breakdown you can read, and sources you can open. That is what we build, one calculator and one guide at a time.</p>
  <h2>How we build a price</h2>
  <p>Every calculator on this site follows the same method. We start with a typical USA job, break it into material, labor, supplies, and equipment, and show a low, typical, and high band for each part. The totals you see are the sum of those parts, so you can check our math line by line.</p>
  <p>Our prices are sample estimated USA ranges built from typical material and labor costs, checked against named public sources such as the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, HUD, Harvard JCHS, Home Depot, and Lowes. Every calculator page shows its own breakdown, so you can see the material, labor, supplies, and equipment behind each total. They are not quotes. Your real price depends on your size, your materials, and where you live, so always get 3 quotes from local pros before you hire anyone.</p>
  <h2>What we do not do</h2>
  <ul class="checklist">
    <li>We do not sell your data. All calculations happen in your browser.</li>
    <li>We do not show prices from outside the USA. Every guide is USA only.</li>
    <li>We do not copy prices from other websites and present them as our own.</li>
  </ul>
  <h2>What the site covers</h2>
  <p>The site covers the jobs Americans price most often before they hire someone. On the home side, that means roof replacement, fences, central air installation, and house painting. On the car side, it means oil changes, car wraps, and car painting. For moving, it means local and long distance moves by home size and distance. Each job has its own calculator and most have a matching guide, so you can get a number fast and then read the detail behind it.</p>
  <p>Every guide opens with a direct answer, shows a price table, and links to the matching calculator. The guides cover what drives the price up or down, what is included in the job, and how to compare quotes from local pros. Prices are USA only, and every range is a sample estimated USA range, not a quote.</p>
  <h2>How to use the calculators and guides</h2>
  <p>Start with the calculator for your job. Pick your size and options, and the page shows a low, typical, and high price in seconds, with an itemized breakdown below it. The low band is a smaller job with basic choices, the typical band is the most common result, and the high band is a larger job, premium choices, or extra services.</p>
  <p>Then read the guide for the same job. The guide shows prices by size, material, or type, and it lists what is included and what is not. Use the calculator result beside 3 written quotes from local pros, and compare the quotes line by line against the breakdown. If a quote is far outside the range, ask the pro what is different before you sign.</p>
  <h2>How prices are checked</h2>
  <p>We build each price from typical material and labor costs, checked against named public sources such as the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, HUD, Harvard JCHS, Home Depot, and Lowes. Each calculator page names its own sources in the references section, so you can see where the figures came from. The totals are the sum of the itemized parts, so you can check the math line by line.</p>
  <p>Prices are checked when a page is updated, and the update date sits at the top of each calculator page. If a price on the site looks wrong for your city or state, tell us through the <a href="{{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}">contact page</a> with the size of the job and the material, and we will check it. Your real price still depends on your size, your materials, and where you live, so always get 3 quotes from local pros before you hire anyone.</p>
  <h2>Who the site is for</h2>
  <p>The site is for adults planning a home, car, or moving job in the USA who want a straight price before they call a pro. It suits a homeowner pricing a roof or a paint job, a driver checking an oil change or a wrap, and anyone planning a move who needs a budget before they book. You do not need an account, and you do not need to sign up. All calculations happen in your browser, and nothing you type into a calculator is sent to our servers or stored.</p>
  <h2>Why you can trust us</h2>
  <p>You can check everything on this site. Every calculator total is the sum of its itemized parts, so you can verify the math line by line. Every calculator and guide names its sources in the references section, and we link only big, high-authority sources such as the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, HUD, Harvard JCHS, Angi, This Old House, Home Depot, and Lowes. Every price is a range, not a quote, because a single number would hide how much size, material, and place change the cost. And if a price looks wrong, tell us through the contact page and we will check it against the sources.</p>
